Lead-in

What Shop Jigs actually are

"Before I get into the tools themselves — quick word on what Shop Jigs even are. These aren't part of the big Workshop Planner, where you're laying out your whole shop. They're small, single-purpose calculators for the math you reach for constantly on the shop floor — converting a metric plan into a fraction you can actually mark on a board, figuring board footage before you buy lumber, getting a miter angle right for a six-sided panel, that kind of thing.

Every one of them opens instantly from the Shop Jigs menu, no matter what page you're on in Kerph — you don't have to leave what you're doing to go find them. They're built to be quick: type a number, get your answer, get back to the bench.

There are eight of them right now, and I'm going to go through each one."
01Measurement Converter
DoOpen the page. Leave it on the default "From millimeters" mode.
Say"This one's for the constant back-and-forth between metric plans, decimal readouts, and the fractions you actually mark on a board."
DoType 19 into the millimeters field.
Say"Type in a millimeter value and it instantly gives you centimeters, decimal inches, and the nearest usable fraction, right down to 1/64th."
DoClick through the "From decimal inches" and "From a fraction" modes.
Say"You've also got two other input modes, so it works whichever direction you're converting."
DoPoint at the sub-line under the fraction result.
Say"Here's the part I actually like — it doesn't just round and move on. It tells you exactly how far off that rounded fraction really is, in both inches and millimeters, so you know if 1/16th is close enough or if you need to switch precision. That precision is adjustable too, down to 1/64th."
02Add & Subtract Fractions
DoOpen the page. Point at the two preloaded lines.
Say"This is for stacking up a running tally the way you'd actually do it at the bench — add this piece, subtract that reveal, add the next piece."
DoClick a line's circle toggle, show it flip from green to red.
Say"Each line is whole inches plus a fraction, and you flip between adding and subtracting with this circle toggle."
DoClick "+ Add Line".
Say"You can keep adding as many lines as the cut list needs, and remove any of them with the X."
DoPoint at the running total at the bottom.
Say"The running total updates live — decimal, millimeters, and the nearest fraction, with the same rounding-error readout as the converter. It's basically a tape measure that does the math for you."
03Board Foot Calculator
DoOpen the page. Fill in thickness, width, and length.
Say"Board feet is thickness times width times length divided by 144 — everybody knows the formula, nobody wants to do it by hand for every board on an order. Type in your dimensions, pick whether length is in feet or inches, and set a quantity."
DoPoint at the row of pill buttons below the inputs, click one (e.g. 8/4).
Say"These are the standard rough-lumber thicknesses — 4/4, 5/4, 6/4, all the way to 12/4 — click one and it sets the thickness field for you."
DoType a price into the $/board foot field, point at Total Cost.
Say"Drop in a price per board foot and it'll total the cost too — useful for a quick materials estimate before you commit to an order."
04Polygon Angle Chart
DoOpen the page. Type 7 into "Number of sides".
Say"Any time you're building something with more than four sides — an octagon planter, a hexagon tabletop — you need three angles: the interior angle, the angle a jig sweeps around the outside corner, and the actual miter angle you set your saw to. Type a side count and it draws the shape and gives you all three instantly, plus the alternate name if the shape has one."
DoScroll down to the reference table.
Say"Below the calculator is a full table, triangle through dodecagon, so you don't even need to type a number for the common ones. And it's worth saying — the miter angle here is the real saw-setting value, not just interior-angle-halved, which is wrong for anything but a square."
05Wood Movement Calculator
DoOpen the page. Open the Species dropdown, show the list.
Say"Wood moves with humidity, and this estimates how much before you build in the tolerance. Pick a species — real species, sixteen of them, from red oak to teak."
DoSet board width. Click "Quartersawn".
Say"Set your board width, choose flatsawn or quartersawn, since quartersawn moves less."
DoClick the relative-humidity toggle.
Say"Then either give it a moisture-content range, or flip to relative humidity if that's what you actually have numbers for — it converts that to moisture content under the hood."
DoPoint at the Movement and New Width results.
Say"The result is expansion or shrinkage in inches, and the new width you should actually build to. It's an estimate, not a guarantee, but it'll keep a panel glue-up from telling on you a year later."
06Speeds & Feeds
DoOpen the page. Type 1.5 into Bit Diameter (first calculator).
Say"Two calculators on this page. First one's router bit max RPM — type a bit diameter and it gives you the safe ceiling, pulled from the standard diameter breakpoint table right below it."
DoScroll to the second calculator. Open Material dropdown, pick Aluminum.
Say"This one's drill press and lathe RPM, and it starts by asking what material you're cutting. It auto-fills a realistic surface speed for that material, but you can still override it by hand."
DoFill in bit/stock diameter, point at Recommended RPM.
Say"Punch in your bit or stock diameter and it gives you the recommended RPM. Good for anyone who's never memorized the SFM numbers for six different materials — which is everybody."
07Sharpening Angle Chart
DoOpen the page. Point at the reference table.
Say"This is just the reference chart I wish was taped to every sharpening station — bevel angles by tool, grouped by category. Paring chisels, general-use chisels, mortise chisels, bench plane irons, carving gouges, turning tools, even a basic pocket knife — all with their typical angle range."
DoScroll down to the Micro-Bevel Helper.
Say"Below that is a small calculator for anyone running a micro-bevel — set your primary bevel angle and the offset you want, and it tells you exactly what angle to set the jig to for that second, narrower bevel."
08Part FinderPro
DoOpen the page. Point at the "Pro" badge.
Say"This one's a Pro feature. You know that drawer of mystery hardware every shop has — a bracket, a weird screw, something that fell off a tool five years ago? Take a photo of it and it identifies the part for you."
DoClick "Take or Choose a Photo", load one, click "Identify This Part".
Say"In a few seconds you get the part name, a confidence level, a description, and specs — and then direct links to buy a match, wherever it's actually available: Amazon, Home Depot, McMaster-Carr, Grainger, Fastenal."
DoPoint at Recent Lookups below.
Say"Every past identification is saved here so you're not re-photographing the same bolt twice. Pro gets three lookups a day, Premier gets twenty."
